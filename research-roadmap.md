# Research roadmap

## Checked results and remaining work

- **Nine separated subset sums:** [The complete real lower bound of 149 is Lean-checked](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/76beffd491584923083c1c3d8f2abe637dde12a3/nine-separated-subset-sums/verification/lean-real-149), with the final axiom audit and a source replay. The exact optimum remains between 149 and 161. The written [uniform rounding theorem](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/785f76b139f2bb7c7110d89839ccdbcad289c6db/nine-separated-subset-sums/verification/uniform-harper-threshold) and [short-gap normalization](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/a49d954ffe00669ed70ac7cdd68ef4091263abbc/nine-separated-subset-sums/verification/rank-cuts-below-150/short-gap-normal-form) are separate results; neither claims 150 for nine.
- **Maximal partial Euler 36-officer arrangements:** The exact minimum is 18 by [independently checked computational proof](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/research/euler-order16/euler-36-officers-disconnected). Lean checks the upper witness and selected size-17 exclusions from geometry all the way to a numbered refutation, including [C04](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/c1dc2b28c48c5d87364e447ceb06e641b08bbe3b/euler-36-officers-disconnected/verification/order17-lean-c04-geometry-link) and the [Pilot diagram](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/832c2e2e7ecd8fe47cf5ebe2edc76c5eaf3fb0ee/euler-36-officers-disconnected/verification/order17-lean-pilot-geometry-link). The full lower bound is not yet end-to-end in Lean.
- **Frankl at height six:** The [written proof excluding exactly four isolates](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/99abc567470ea8669712272bee26b25a81a0b6ee/frankl-height-six/verification/at-least-five-isolates) implies that a minimum-ground weak counterexample must have at least five isolates. The newest proof is not Lean-checked; the general problem is open.
- **Width-three posets:** The [longer and crossed two-arm cases](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/6add80bc727aef16d920be7ce39d8312fed99a87/width-three-posets/verification/conditional-xyz-two-arm-slack) have written balanced-pair proofs. They are restricted classes, not a proof of the general conjecture.
- **Erdős–Szekeres at 33 points:** An [independent exact audit](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/e34e6924be9c40322019132c4613fd241ccdef13/erdos-szekeres-33/verification/exact-guarded-clause-audit) checks all 474,144 guarded clauses of the anchored 32-label target. The target is not settled, and the concrete bridge has not been proved in Lean.
- **Hadwiger–Nelson and G449:** [Every extension of G449 by up to six new points is five-colorable](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/294c4596476e933f75703479fab2ea0f87e3723e/hadwiger-nelson/verification/g449/prime-seven-catalogue/six-point-extension-theorem), by exact computational proof. This is a result for that fixed graph, not Lean verification or a new lower or upper bound on the chromatic number of the plane.
- **P vs NP contribution:** [Helper lemmas and limited circuit architectures](https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/research/geometry-and-complexity/pequalnp-contribution) are checked. The assigned general lower bound and P vs NP remain open here.

## Problem statements and context

**Hadwiger–Nelson.** Does coloring the Euclidean plane so that points at distance one receive different colors take six colors or seven? The published research release gives a Lean-checked obstruction to five colors; the exact value is still listed as open in [Formal Conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/508.lean). Aim for a genuine six-coloring or an explicit finite unit-distance graph that requires seven. A separate constructive milestone is an explicit graph that is not five-colorable; that would make the known lower bound constructive, not resolve six versus seven. See the [five-color obstruction](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-Euclidean-plane-is-not-five-colorable-September-23-2026) and the [November 2026 finite five-chromatic construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28518).

**Erdős–Szekeres: the 33-point problem.** Must every set of 33 points in the plane, no three collinear, contain seven points in convex position? This is the first unresolved exact case. An explicit set of 33 points avoiding such a heptagon would disprove the classical prediction; a proof no such set exists settles this case. The most useful intermediate target is a checkable reduction of the possible point-order configurations, not a floating-point search. [33-point problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24061) · [November 2026 general upper-bound advance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29344).

**Collatz.** Repeatedly halve even positive integers and replace odd ones by three times the number plus one. Must every starting integer reach 1? A meaningful partial goal is a uniform new bound on a precisely defined family of trajectories or cycles, not merely more tested starting values. [Tao's almost-all theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562) and [recent computational work](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-026-00811-5) set separate theoretical and numerical baselines.

**Caccetta–Häggkvist, directed triangles.** Must an oriented graph on n vertices with at least n/3 outgoing edges at every vertex have a directed triangle? This is an accessible statement with a substantial graph theory payoff. A possible first result is a structural theorem reducing the search for triangle-free near-extremal configurations. See [recent work on directed cycles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22909) and [the flag-algebra approach to triangles](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2791).

**P vs NP.** A solution would be a landmark, but the gap between a useful restricted result and the full question is enormous. I would not make it the first project without a specific circuit or proof-complexity target. [Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/).
