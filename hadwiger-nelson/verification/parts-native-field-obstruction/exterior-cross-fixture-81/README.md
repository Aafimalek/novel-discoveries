# A small exact Parts-style mixed-edge fixture

Let s=√−3, r=√33, and j=√−15, with imaginary roots chosen positive.
Write ω=(1+s)/2, Λ=Z[ω], and W={0}∪{ω^k:0≤k<6}. Let T=W+W, the
19-site radius-two triangular lattice ball. Put

    σ = (5+√−11)/6 = (5+sr/3)/6,
    ρ = (7+j)/8,
    B = T ∪ σT,
    F = B ∪ ρB.

Here σ,ρ have norm one. As σ∉Q(s), the two copies in B intersect
only at zero, so |B|=37. As ρ∉K=Q(s,r), the two copies of B also
intersect only at zero, so |F|=73. For the last assertion, the
three square classes -3,33,-15 are independent: the exponents
of 11 and 5 first eliminate the latter two classes, and -3 is
not a rational square.

## Mixed edges and limitations of additive coloring

For every sixth root u, the radius-two sites 2u and 2ρu belong
to F and form an exact unit edge:

    |2u−2ρu|² = 4(2−2·7/8) = 1.

These are genuine cross-copy edges: the corresponding displacement
has both independent K summands nonzero in K+ρK. This is the
reference-radius mechanism of [Parts, arXiv:2010.12665v2, §4,
subtypes of type M](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.12665v2#S4),
not a novel edge mechanism. The earlier α/β product coloring
does not apply: these are mixed steps, not just ways to choose
new independent summands.

The first native block already contains the Moser spindle.
The two diamonds have common endpoint 0, respective far endpoints
s and σs, and respective middle vertices (s±1)/2 and σ(s±1)/2.
All occur in B because s, (s±1)/2∈T. Their far endpoints are
unit separated since |s−σs|²=3(2−2·5/6)=1. As the middle
vertices of each diamond are adjacent, any three-coloring
forces the two diamonds' far endpoints to share 0's color,
contradicting the last edge. The seven sites are distinct by
their radii and the fact that the middle directions differ by
60 degrees while the rotation angle is strictly between zero
and 60 degrees.

There is also no proper **additive** map, even on the additive
group generated by F, to any group of at most four colors.
Orders at most three are excluded by the spindle. A map to
the two-dimensional binary group kills every doubled
displacement, in particular 2−2ρ. If the group is cyclic
of order 4, the edges for u=1,ω,ω−1 require the images
of 1−ρ, (1−ρ)ω, and (1−ρ)(ω−1) all to be odd, since
their doubles must be nonzero mod 4. All three underlying
differences belong to the generated additive group. But
the third is the difference of the first two, so cannot
also be odd. This does not rule out nonadditive four-coloring.

## Auxiliary control not tested; 81-site pilot

For an unequal-radius auxiliary join (the same Parts §4 mechanism)
one can include the sites p and q in B before duplicating it,
where p=i(√11/2+√3/6), q=i(√11/2−√3/6). Both are in K.
They are distinct nonzero sites lying on the same ray, with
|p|²+|q|²=17/3 and |p|·|q|=8/3. Thus p and ρq have squared
distance 17/3−2·(8/3)·7/8=1. This would add the two reference
sites and their two copies. These auxiliary sites were not
added in the computation below.

One extra rotation can close the analogous order-five additive
shortcut, without making any nonadditive claim. Starting from
F, add 3,4,5 and kτ for 1≤k≤5, with

    τ=(49+3√−11)/50.

This adds eight distinct sites, connected to 0 by unit chains
in directions 1 and τ, and yields the unit edge 5↔5τ. Indeed
|5−5τ|²=25(2−2·49/50)=1. The ratio τ/σ is nonreal
(it is not ±1) and belongs to Q(√−11), whose intersection
with Q(s) is Q; τ itself is also outside Q(s).
Hence the τ-chain does not collide with T or σT, and no
nonzero τ-chain site is in the exterior copy ρB since
it lies in K. The new real sites have radius above two.
The additive group generated by these 81 sites contains 1−τ,
so an additive map to order 5 kills the edge 5(1−τ). Therefore
the 81-site extension rules out all additive maps to groups
of at most five colors. The scope is a homomorphism defined
on the additive group generated by all included sites (or on
any larger additive group), followed if desired by relabeling
of colors or a global color shift. It is not all ordinary
graph colorings.

## Exact pilot and witness

Only the 81-site extension was generated and tested. Its
coordinate set is regenerated as both wheel Minkowski sums
and exact triangular lattice hexagonal balls; all sites
are distinct as elements of the bitmask basis of
Q(√−3,√33,√−15), with common denominator 7200.
All 3,240 unordered squared norms were checked both by
generic radical multiplication and by splitting into real
and imaginary parts over Q(√33,√5). Exactly 201 are unit
edges. The separate model checker recomputes the complete
edge set directly from the real/imaginary formula and
validates every unit edge against the returned coloring.

A single ordinary four-color SAT test found an explicit
proper coloring. Together with the embedded Moser spindle,
this proves ordinary chromatic number exactly four.
Hence despite the additive obstruction there is no
five-color forced-difference pair in this fixture: give
any one chosen nonadjacent pair a fresh fifth color.
The archived model checker confirms this directly for the
marked terminals 0 and 2, including their exact squared
distance. No other fixture, Lean, or Git was run.
