\begin{theorem}
Let \(G\) be the fixed
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28518}{Heule--Przybocki G449}
with its exact unit-distance geometry. For every original vertex
\(x\) and every new point \(z\) anywhere in the plane with
\(|x-z|=2\), there is a proper five-coloring of the extended
unit-distance graph in which \(x\) and \(z\) have the same color.
\end{theorem}

The finite color bank is taken from the
\href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/294c4596476e933f75703479fab2ea0f87e3723e/hadwiger-nelson/verification/g449/prime-seven-catalogue/six-point-extension-theorem}{exact six-point extension work}.
All 61 original color vectors are directly checked on every
original edge. In this bank, every pair of distinct originals
has a palette giving them different colors. This covers an
added point with at most one original neighbor: if the neighbor
exists it differs from \(x\), since \(|x-z|=2\), and one chooses
a palette coloring that neighbor differently from \(x\).
Assign \(z\) the color of \(x\).

\paragraph{Grid points of original degree at least two.}
Use the complete verified signed unit-step catalogue for
\(\tfrac17\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{42}]\) to regenerate all 7,056
candidate sites and their original neighbor lists. Scan the
3,168,144 original-to-candidate pairs. For the integer
coefficient vector \(d\) of a displacement scaled by seven,
the distance-two test (distance exactly two, squared
norm four) is the exact cyclotomic remainder condition
\(d(X)d(X^{-1})\equiv196\pmod{\Phi_{42}(X)}\).
Evaluation at checked primitive order-42 roots modulo 43 and
127 is a necessary filter; exactly 896 pairs pass both.
Integer polynomial reduction of all 896 gives 861 true pairs,
of which 539 are not reflections through an original midpoint.
Every true pair has a checked base palette leaving the color of
\(x\) absent at all original neighbors of \(z\).

\paragraph{Off-grid points.}
The earlier
\href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/6376de4361b98f605326dd6275fa5f9e8b5dd3fb/hadwiger-nelson/verification/g449/prime-seven-catalogue/six-off-grid-extensions}{off-grid neighbor bound}
shows that a point outside the grid has at most two original
neighbors. If there are two, call them \(y,w\). Neither is
\(x\). For a distinguished original \(x\) and any distinct
\(y,w\ne x\), write \(D(x,y)\) for the bitset of proper palettes
where the first pair differs. The exact check covers all
\(449\binom{448}{2}=44{,}957{,}472\) such distinguished triples;
in every case \(D(x,y)\cap D(x,w)\ne\varnothing\).
With a palette from that intersection, give \(z\) the color
of \(x\). This off-grid argument in fact applies at any
nonunit distance from \(x\), not just distance two.

\medskip\noindent
Both exact replays were independently repeated. No SAT solver
or floating-point predicate is part of the proof. It rules out a
single attached distance-two terminal as a forced-inequality
gadget on this base, not larger gadgets or a six-coloring of
the plane. This is not a Lean formalization.
