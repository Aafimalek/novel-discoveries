\textbf{Theorem.} The Heule--Przybocki graph \(G_{449}\) remains
five-colorable after adding six points in the denominator-seven
cyclotomic grid if at least one new point has no original neighbor.
The proof uses the
\href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/234e0ce194fdfa544d4b551ccea9898b2239dc69/hadwiger-nelson/verification/g449/prime-seven-catalogue/five-point-extensions}{five-point extension theorem}
and the complete exact replay described below. This does not settle
every six-point extension or improve the chromatic bounds of the plane.

\section{Scope and completeness}\label{scope-and-completeness}

Assume the earlier arbitrary five-point extension theorem. For a
six-point extension write \(b(v)\) for degree in the original 449 points
and \(d_H(v)\) for degree among the six new points. A counterexample
satisfies \(\delta_{\rm add}:=\min_v (b(v)+d_H(v))\ge5\), by deleting
and greedily restoring any vertex with at most four total neighbors.

If \(b(x)=0\), then \(d_H(x)=5\): \(x\) is adjacent to all five rim
points. No other new point can have \(b\le1\): two distinct points have
at most two common unit neighbors, so every other point has at most two
rim neighbors on the unit circle around \(x\), hence at most three new
neighbors including \(x\). The other five therefore each have \(b\ge2\).
Their candidate set is the 7,056 outside grid points having at least two
original neighbors. The 546 signed exact directions generate all
3,852,576 candidate rim-to-hub incidences. Exclude original points and
points with any original neighbor. Shards partition the remaining
candidate hubs by a fixed hash of their exact coefficient key;
\textbf{all incidences for a given hub go to the same shard}.

For each candidate hub, use exact displacement membership for all rim
adjacency. On a unit circle, unit edges move by \(\pm60^\circ\), so the
full available unit-circle graph has components of at most six vertices
and maximum degree two. Selected sets of five are disjoint paths. A
selected rim \(v\) must have \(b(v)+d_{\rm rim}(v)\ge4\). Iteratively
discard a rim site that cannot meet that inequality even with every
remaining possible neighbor; by induction, this does not discard a rim
from an eligible set. Within each component enumerate all nonempty
subsets of size at most five that meet the inequality for every selected
rim. Then combine subsets from disjoint components in increasing
component order to size five, skipping components as needed. This
enumerates all eligible rim configurations exactly once. The program
also records the unpruned sum of
\(\binom{|\mathrm{available\ rim}|}{5}\) over hubs.

For every bank coloring, derive each rim color list by removing the
colors at its actual base neighbors. Try all five colors at the
degree-zero hub and remove the chosen color from each rim list. An exact
finite list solver propagates assignments and tries all remaining colors
if necessary. Any positive result is a proper five-coloring of the
induced extension. A configuration not covered by this bank is merely a
residual, not a proof of six-chromaticity.

\section{Complete replay}
We verified all 32 hub shards, covering 3,852,576 grid incidences.
After excluding the original points and their neighbors, there are
3,384,633 possible hubs. Of these, 15,638 have at least five rim
sites, giving 715,925 raw choices of five. Minimum degree leaves
4,578 eligible configurations, all covered by the checked coloring
bank; the residual set is empty.
\par
The source and input checksums permit a fresh C++20 replay.
The first 25 verified palettes sufficed. No new SAT solve or
Lean proof is used in this verification.
