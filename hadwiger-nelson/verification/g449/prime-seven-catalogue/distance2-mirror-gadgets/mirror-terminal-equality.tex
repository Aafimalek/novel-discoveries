\maketitle
A unit-distance graph with distance-two terminals forced to
receive different colors in every five-coloring could turn
two-distance obstructions with edge lengths one and two into
unit-distance obstructions, by replacing each length-two
edge with a copy of the gadget. This note rules out one
concrete candidate family, not the gadget approach in general.

\paragraph{Claim.}
Let \(G\) be the exact
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28518}{Heule--Przybocki G449}.
Take any oriented original unit edge \(x\to y\), let \(z=2y-x\),
and optionally add any further distinct point \(w\) in the
verified grid \(\tfrac17\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{42}]\), outside the
original vertices and \(z\). Include all unit edges. The
result has a proper five-coloring in which \(x\) and \(z\)
have the same color. They are at distance two. The statement
does not cover arbitrary terminals or off-grid catalysts.

\paragraph{Exact checks.}
Checking all 100,576 pairs of original coordinates by
cyclotomic multiplication shows that none is at distance two.
Thus all 4,396 proposed reflected sites are outside the base.
They are in fact distinct. Their original-neighbor counts
are 4,074 with one, 273 with two, 28 with three, and 21 with
four. Using the 61 checked base colorings from the
\href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/294c4596476e933f75703479fab2ea0f87e3723e/hadwiger-nelson/verification/g449/prime-seven-catalogue/six-point-extension-theorem}{six-point extension theorem},
each original terminal color is allowed at its reflected partner
under at least one palette.

In the presence of \(w\), the extension is greedy whenever
\(w\) has at most four already colored neighbors. Otherwise
\(w\) has at least four original neighbors if adjacent to \(z\),
or at least five if nonadjacent. The complete
denominator-seven direction catalogue leaves exactly 1,043
outside grid sites with at least four original neighbors.
For a fixed terminal pair let \(A_c\) be the set of palettes
giving both terminals color \(c\), and let \(B_c(w)\) be the
palettes allowing \(w\) some color other than \(c\).
If \(z,w\) are adjacent, find a palette in some
\(A_c\cap B_c(w)\). Otherwise find a palette in some
\(A_c\) under which \(w\) has a nonempty list.
The exact program selects and checks one positive palette
and color in every tested case.

There are 4,585,028 reflected-terminal--high-degree
catalyst pairs. Of these, 21 are not admissible because
\(w=z\), and 2,061,031 nonadjacent degree-four cases are
greedy. The exact mask replay verifies all 1,057 remaining
adjacent cases and 2,522,919 remaining nonadjacent cases,
with zero palette misses. All other grid catalysts are
covered greedily. No SAT exit status or floating point
predicate contributes to the conclusion.


\paragraph{A second restriction.}
The ordered list of colors under these 61 proper palettes is
different at each of the 449 original vertices. The exact replay
checks a differing-color palette for every original vertex pair.
Thus the original graph has no pair of distinct vertices forced to
receive the same color under all five-colorings. This eliminates
another way of making an attached new terminal differ in color at
distance two, but not all such gadgets.
\medskip\noindent
This is an exact computational negative result for a restricted
construction. It is not Lean-checked and does not change
the known bounds on the chromatic number of the plane.
