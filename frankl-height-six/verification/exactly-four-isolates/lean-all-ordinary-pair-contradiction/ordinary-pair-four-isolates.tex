We close one multiple-source branch of the height-six
union-closed families project, with an end-to-end Lean proof.
The distinction from the full conjecture is the ordinary-pair
hypothesis on omitted-star blocks.

\begin{theorem}
A weak minimum-ground family in the project's height-six
setting cannot have exactly four isolated points and at least
two sources when every omitted-star block of a distinct
source pair has size two.
\end{theorem}

\section*{Counting in an anchored pair}
Let \(k\) be the number of sources, \(Q=\binom k2\), and let
\(G\) be such a family. The forcing relation records the
elements that must occur together in the indicated direction.
A source and its proper forced leaves form a star; an isolated
point belongs to no nontrivial forcing relation. In the
present four-isolate multiple-source case, every star is
unary: an anchor to a multileaf star would give a pair
avoidance fiber supported on the four isolates, containing
at most five members since every family member has size
at least three. This contradicts its baseline avoidance
count. Thus the ground has \(2k+4\) points.

Fix distinct sources \(p,q\) and consider the fiber of members
avoiding their chosen leaves. The omitted-star blocks are
exactly all unordered pairs of sources. The baseline counts
the ordinary avoidance witnesses, the distinct source
witnesses, and all \(\binom{k-2}{2}\) blocks away from the
anchor. Write \(K\) for any extra list of distinct mixed
witness indices \((r,J)\), where \(r\) is an exterior source and
\(J\) a nonempty isolate hole. A mixed witness is the ground
with precisely that unary star and those isolates omitted.
The Lean-checked disjointness count is
\[
 |\text{fiber}|+1\;\ge\; (2k+4)+k+\binom{k-2}{2}+|K|,
 \qquad
 |\text{fiber}|\;\ge\;Q+k+6+|K|.
\]
Different source-hole indices give different members, and none
is counted by the baseline.

Compress the fiber to the nonomitted star centers and present
isolates, and complement in its true support. Its cardinality
is unchanged. The result is an intersection-closed family
containing the empty set and no chain of four distinct members.
Its support has size at most \(m=k+2\). If it has \(a\)
inclusion-minimal nonempty members, its size is at most
\(1+m+\binom a2\) by the atom-counting bound. Its absolute
capacity is \(H(m)=1+m+\binom m2\).

\section*{Mixed witnesses force saturation}
For \(k\ge3\), the baseline forces \(a\ge m-1\): if \(a\le
m-2=k\), the upper bound is \(Q+k+3\). The atoms are disjoint.
If all support coordinates are present, there are at least
three singleton isolate omissions, or two singleton isolate
omissions together with a double atom containing the other
two isolates. If one support coordinate is missing, it must
be an isolate. All true atoms are then singletons, giving
absolute isolate omissions consisting of the missing isolate
by itself and its pairs with each other isolate. No other
support or atom type fits \(m-1\) disjoint atoms. In all three
patterns the union of the available nonempty isolate holes
contains at least three of the four isolates.

Take two incident pair blocks through an exterior source
\(r\). The two hole lists have at least one isolate in common.
Choosing a hole containing it from each list and unioning the
actual fiber members gives a genuine mixed witness: the
intersection of omitted stars is precisely the star at
\(r\), and the intersection of the isolate holes is nonempty.
This yields at least \(k-2\) extra witnesses for every anchor.
If \(a\le m-1\) its capacity is at most \(Q+2k+3\), but the
new count is at least \(Q+2k+4\). So all \(m\) atoms occur and
are singletons on full support. Each exterior source now
provides four mixed witnesses, one for every isolate singleton.

For \(k\ge4\), those \(4(k-2)\) witnesses exceed the available
slack \(H(k+2)-(Q+k+6)=2k-2\). For \(k=3\), every fiber must
instead have exact size \(H(5)=16\). With all singleton
atoms, different nonsingleton members cannot share a coordinate
pair, since their intersection would be a proper
nonsingleton member. Equality forces every pair to occur
as its own member. Thus two incident anchors at the exterior
source allow the same double-isolate hole. Their union
gives an additional fifth mixed witness, so the anchored
fiber must have at least 17 members, contradiction.

Finally, if \(k=2\), the ground has size eight and the anchor
omits both two-point stars. The avoiding fiber lives on
at most four points, so it has at most five members, whereas
the baseline is nine. This finishes the ordinary-pair branch.
