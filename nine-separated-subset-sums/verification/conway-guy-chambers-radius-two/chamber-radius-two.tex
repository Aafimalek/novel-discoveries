\section{Exact local chamber obstruction at the nine-weight Conway--Guy
witness}\label{exact-local-chamber-obstruction-at-the-nine-weight-conwayguy-witness}

Let \(a=(77,117,137,148,154,157,159,160,161)\) be the Conway--Guy
witness. For each nonzero \(r\in\{-1,0,1\}^9\), choose the unique
orientation with \(r\cdot a>0\), once per \(\{r,-r\}\). Thus there are
9,841 oriented signed relations. An ordered unit-separated \(x\) is in a
chamber when it satisfies \(r\cdot x\ge1\) for each designated positive
relation and \(-r\cdot x\ge1\) for a designated flip. This is equivalent
to all 512 labeled subset sums being unit separated with the given
order. Distances here count \emph{signed relation pairs}, not pairwise
subset comparisons (several literal comparisons can use the same signed
word), not Euclidean distance.

\textbf{Result.} No sorted chamber whose signs differ from \(a\) in at
most two oriented relations can improve its maximum below 161. In
particular each of the six genuine single-wall neighbors that reverse
one of the first six Conway--Guy dual rows is excluded by a small
explicit positive dual, without an LP solve. This does not rule out
distant chambers.

\subsection{Starting representation and exact tight
words}\label{starting-representation-and-exact-tight-words}

By the specialization at \(n=9\) of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17360}{Notik's Theorem 3.2}, there is
the exact identity \(x_9=\sum_{j=1}^9 y_jR_j(x)\) of linear forms, with
the following rows and positive weights. The table uses a dot for a zero
coordinate.

\begin{longtable}[]{@{}llr@{}}
\toprule\noalign{}
Row & Signed word & Weight \\
\midrule\noalign{}
\endhead
\bottomrule\noalign{}
\endlastfoot
\(R_1\) & ++++.-.-- & 1 \\
\(R_2\) & -++++-.-- & 1 \\
\(R_3\) & .-+++..-- & 2 \\
\(R_4\) & ..-+++.-- & 4 \\
\(R_5\) & \ldots-+++-- & 8 \\
\(R_6\) & \ldots.-++.- & 15 \\
\(R_7\) & \ldots..-++- & 25 \\
\(R_8\) & \ldots\ldots-+. & 48 \\
\(R_9\) & \ldots\ldots.-+ & 57 \\
\end{longtable}

Every listed row is CG-positive and evaluates to 1 at \(a\). The weights
sum to 161. The checker loops over the complete \(3^9\) ternary words,
including the zero word, then orients all nonzero ones by their dot
product with \(a\). There are exactly 12 CG-positive words of value 1.
The three additional words are \(E_j=R_7+R_9-R_j\) for \(j=1,2,3\), and
are respectively \texttt{-\/-\/-\/-..+++}, \texttt{+-\/-\/-\/-.+++},
\texttt{.+-\/-\/-\/-+++}. This classification is checked by full exact
enumeration. The proof of sign-distance obstruction does not depend on
having found every tight word; the classification is included for
reproducibility and to describe all tight neighbors.

\subsection{Genuine neighboring sign
chambers}\label{genuine-neighboring-sign-chambers}

For \emph{any} tight CG-positive word \(r\), put
\(s=\lVert r\rVert_2^2\) (its support size) and \(a_*=a-r/s\). Then
\(r\cdot a_*=0\). For any other CG-positive word \(u\), \(u\cdot a\) is
a positive integer and \(u\cdot r\le s\). Equality \(u\cdot a_*=0\)
would force \(u\cdot a=1\) and \(u\) to agree with \(r\) on every
nonzero coordinate of \(r\). Then \(u-r\) would be a nonzero ternary
word supported in the zero coordinates of \(r\) with \((u-r)\cdot a=0\),
impossible by distinct subset sums. Thus all the other oriented words
are strictly positive at \(a_*\).

More explicitly take \(\eta=1/(2s)\) and \(a'=a-(1+\eta)r/s\). The
flipped word has value \(r\cdot a'=-\eta\). Every other tight word
satisfies \(u\cdot r\le s-1\), so
\(u\cdot a'\ge1-(1+\eta)(s-1)/s>\eta\). Every CG-positive word of value
at least 2 at \(a\) has \(u\cdot a'\ge1-\eta\ge\eta\). Therefore
\(2s\,a'\) has the single flipped orientation and is unit separated.
This is an explicit rational existence witness for the adjacent sign
chamber, not a low maximum witness. For \(R_1,\ldots,R_7\) the sorted
signs remain positive (the changed word is not an adjacent
singleton-order relation). The checker also independently evaluates
every oriented row and sorts all 512 rational subset scores for each
such witness.

\subsection{All single changes are blocked by written positive
duals}\label{all-single-changes-are-blocked-by-written-positive-duals}

If a unique sign flip is not one of the nine \(R_j\), their original
positive identity still gives \(x_9\ge161\), whether or not the chosen
non-tight word is actually a chamber wall. Any exact equality with their
unchanged constraints would force \(R_j(x)=1\) for all \(j\); Notik's
uniqueness in the original nine-row system then gives \(x=a\), which
cannot realize any sign change.

If \(R_j\) alone flips for \(j=1,\ldots,7\), put \(k=8\) for \(j\le5\)
and \(k=9\) for \(j=6,7\). The word \(U_j=R_j+R_k\) is itself ternary
and has value 2 at \(a\), so it keeps its positive sign. Replace the
\(j,k\) terms in the original identity by the exact combination \[
 (y_k-y_j)(-R_j)+y_kU_j=y_jR_j+y_kR_k .
\] Both coefficients are nonnegative. Therefore the cap lower bound is
\(161+y_k-2y_j\), respectively \(207,207,205,201,193,188,168\). These
need not be the exact optima of the neighboring chambers; they suffice
to reject a cap of 161. Reversing \(R_8=x_8-x_7\) or \(R_9=x_9-x_8\) is
incompatible with the increasing order. For the extra tight flips
\(E_j\), the identity \(R_j=R_7+R_9-E_j\) even strengthens the unchanged
dual bound to \(161+2y_j\).

\subsection{Simultaneous two signs (static exact
extension)}\label{simultaneous-two-signs-static-exact-extension}

The only new cases to cover have exactly one or two basis rows \(R_j\)
flipped (only indices 1 through 7 are possible).

If two flipped rows are both in 1 through 5, their two CG-positive
\(U\)'s can both use the common \(R_8\) budget: allocate nonnegative
amounts \(z_i,z_j\) with \(z_i+z_j=48\), \(z_i\ge y_i\), and
\(z_j\ge y_j\). The same substitution gives the bound
\(161+48-2(y_i+y_j)\ge185\). If one is in 1 through 5 and the other in 6
or 7, their ordinary substitutions use disjoint \(R_8,R_9\) budgets. For
the pair 6,7, instead use \(U'_6=R_6+R_8\) for 6 and \(U_7=R_7+R_9\) for
7, again disjoint. All words here are legitimate ternary words of CG
value 2 and are not either flipped basis word.

If just \(R_j\) is flipped among the basis, the earlier dual covers any
second flip other than its companion \(U_j\). For \(j\le5\), the
twice-flipped case also has the ternary word \(V_j=R_j+R_8+R_9=U_j+R_9\)
(CG value 3) still positive. Use the exact substitution \[
y_jR_j+48R_8+57R_9
=(48-y_j)R_8+(57-y_j)(-U_j)+57V_j .
\] This gives the lower bound \(161+57-3y_j>161\). For \(j=6\), use the
other companion \(U'_6\) instead, yielding \(179\). For \(j=7\),
\(U_7=x_7-x_6\) is positive for every sorted candidate, so it cannot be
the second flip. These exhaust the two-sign cases.

\subsection{Exact replay}\label{exact-replay}

\begin{verbatim}
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
python3 check_neighbors.py
python3 check_two_sign.py
\end{verbatim}

Optionally, to materialize the six complete all-sign LP inputs without
running any LP solver:

\begin{verbatim}
python3 check_neighbors.py --emit-lp ./six-models
\end{verbatim}

The checker enforces every one of the 9,841 exact sign rows in each
rational witness, verifies all 512 subset scores independently, and
checks that every lower dual sums to the objective with nonnegative
coefficients on enforced signs. The two-sign replay verifies a finite
family of exact algebraic dual substitutions; all other sign patterns in
radius two are covered by the reduction in the written proof. No
numerical optimization or Lean check is claimed.
