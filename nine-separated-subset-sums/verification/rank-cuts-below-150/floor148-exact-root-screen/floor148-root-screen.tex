Let \(0<x_1<\cdots<x_9\) have pairwise unit-separated subset sums, with
\(149\le M=x_9<150\), and put \(X=x_8\) and \(d=M-X\).
The \href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/c582f2587588eeeb195048a74552fe80bdc1d0e7/nine-separated-subset-sums/verification/rank-cuts-below-150/only-floor-148}{preceding rank-swap proof}
gives \(148\le X<149\) and \(39(d-1)\le M-148\).
Let \(s\in\{-1,1\}^9\) be the oriented difference between the upper
and lower middle subsets; they are complements, so every coordinate of
\(s\) is nonzero.
\textbf{Result:} exact certificates rule out 388 of the 512 possible
orientations \(s\), including all 316 excluded by the earlier root and
rank-moment tests. The remaining 124 orientations are undecided by
this computation, and no lower bound of 150 is claimed.

\paragraph{Additional inequalities.}
For any positive difference \(e\) of two first-seven subset sums, we
have \(e\ge1\) directly and \(\lvert e-d\rvert\ge1\) by translating the
two subsets by \(X\) and \(M\), respectively. Since \(d-1<1\), this
forces \(e\ge1+d\). In particular, \(x_1\ge1+d\) and
\(x_{i+1}-x_i\ge1+d\) for \(1\le i\le6\).
The other new rows are \(-X\le-148\) and \(38M-39X\le-109\).
The replay appends these nine rows to the prior orientation, matching,
packing and rank-moment inequalities. It keeps the original strict rows
\(M<150\) and \(s\cdot x<2\).

\paragraph{Exact exclusion and replay.}
For each rejected orientation the archive supplies nonnegative rational
multipliers \(y\) for its rows \(Ax\le b\) (with the designated rows
strict). The checker reconstructs \(A\) and \(b\) and verifies
\(A^\top y=0\) and either \(b^\top y<0\), or \(b^\top y=0\)
with positive weight on a strict row. Summation yields \(0<0\) or
\(0\le b^\top y<0\). Only this arithmetic counts as an
exclusion. The solver used to suggest multipliers is not required for
replay. All 64 disjoint eight-orientation shards are included; no
slot-sign constraints or scaled lattice cuts are used. A surviving
orientation is not evidence of a valid configuration. The archive ships
the reconstructed rows, replay code, and earlier certificates used for
the exact comparison. This check is not a Lean formalization.
