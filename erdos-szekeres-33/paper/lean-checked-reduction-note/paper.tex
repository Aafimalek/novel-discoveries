\begin{abstract}
We formalize a forward reduction of the real 33-point convex heptagon
problem to a particular numbered Boolean formula generated inside Lean.
Any real counterexample yields a satisfying assignment to this formula,
including all auxiliary chain variables. We separately check, by parsing
literal bytes, that six clauses of an external solver input occur in the
internal formula. Neither a correspondence for the full external file nor
an unsatisfiability certificate is asserted.
\end{abstract}

\section*{The exact statement}
Let \(T\) be the numbered formula defined in Lean by
\[
  \mathtt{compile}\;(\mathtt{dictionary}\ 32\ 7\ 6)\;
                 (\mathtt{typedRows}\ 32\ 7\ 7\ 6)\;
                 \mathtt{target32\_covered}.
\]
The final argument is the checked dictionary coverage theorem, not an
additional hypothesis on the point configuration. The formula is the
base positive-anchor target without external reflection-lex augmentation.

\begin{theorem}
If \(T\) has no satisfying assignment, every set of 33 distinct real plane
points with no three collinear has seven points in convex position.
Equivalently in the forward direction used here, any real counterexample
to that statement supplies a satisfying assignment to \(T\).
\end{theorem}

The formal endpoint is
\texttt{ES33.EndpointConcrete.es33\_of\_internal\_target32\_unsat}.
Its geometry is expressed for an embedding of \(\mathrm{Fin}(33)\) into the
real plane with general position. The output is a seven-element finite
subset of the image in convex position. The proof and its axiom audit are
in the \href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/e8b089e7a3f484553d2b2660e30779ded0e83e04/erdos-szekeres-33/verification/lean-end-to-end-internal-target-reduction}{checked endpoint release}.
A \href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/57e521a334d4078ee36acf7fd8d1b78b4a9f9411/erdos-szekeres-33/verification/lean-end-to-end-internal-target-reduction/portable}{portable
33-source bundle} gives the local transitive dependency order and matching
historical receipts. Strict replay uses Lean 4.36.0. The endpoint uses
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\section*{Proof structure}
A real counterexample supplies an ordered real witness on 32 points by
the checked anchor reduction. Its determinant signs obey the signotope
packet rules, forbid the relevant even seven-set, and forbid an
all-positive six-set. For those signs, assign each endpoint auxiliary
variable by actual existence of a cap or cup of the specified length.
This is one consistent valuation, not independent choices for separate
clauses. The chain recurrence proves all required guarded propagation.

Three geometric checks give the exclusions. Opposite cap and cup
chains sharing their distinct endpoints intersect in precisely the
two endpoints. For a selected triple in their union its sign equals the
sign attached to its median, by homogeneity on each chain and the
local packet rules between the chains. Consequently the first two
signs and the last two signs of every ordered quartet match in pairs.
Chains of sizes \(s,t\) give an even set of size \(s+t-2\); when
\(s+t=9\) this contradicts the seven-set exclusion. A seven-cap is
handled with the trivial opposite two-cup on its endpoints. A six-cup
directly gives the forbidden all-positive six-set. These arguments apply
to the same chain valuation for which propagation was checked.

Finally, checked constructor and dictionary coverage transport the
guarded rows to the actual signed-number compiler. There are no
remaining assumptions on auxiliary variables in the theorem.
The proof is one-way: an abstract SAT assignment need not be a
realizable order type.

\section*{External file pilot}
The \texttt{parsed\_packet\_mem\_internal} theorem verifies six exact
embedded clause-body lines. Lean checks a string-to-ASCII witness for
those bytes, parses signed integer tokens and line delimiters, derives
the live production sign IDs \(1,2,31,466\), and proves literal membership
of every resulting row in \(T\). This is not justified by matching
hashes or by a pre-parsed table. The bound \(37\,365\) is the token
parser's declared bound for this fixture. The fixture extraction notes
identify the corresponding six consecutive body lines in the external
file; the global file position is not a Lean theorem.


The released fixture is exactly the following text, with single spaces
and LF line terminators:
\begin{verbatim}
-1 -466 2 0
-2 -466 31 0
-1 -31 2 0
1 466 -2 0
2 466 -31 0
1 31 -2 0
\end{verbatim}
Zero is the clause delimiter, not a Boolean variable. The IDs are
established from the live dictionary's prefix, not a separately assigned
numbering for this example. See the \href{https://github.com/Aafimalek/novel-discoveries/tree/9f014516761d46f011b85037eab5d5bcac3ba585/erdos-szekeres-33/verification/lean-exact-external-six-clause-pilot}{checked six-clause release}
for the literal bytes, the Lean parsing proof, and the membership audit.

For a solver proof of \(T\), one sufficient bridge would show that every
clause used in the precise externally parsed checker formula occurs in
\(T\). UNSAT of that checker formula would then imply UNSAT of \(T\).
Neither this whole-input containment nor a checked UNSAT certificate has
been established. The 33-point conjecture is therefore not solved here.
